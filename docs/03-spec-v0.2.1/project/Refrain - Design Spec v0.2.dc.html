Capabilities, round two — closing the gaps, deepening the instrument

The Maestro shows its work.

Round one proved the thesis: the code is the score, and every AI action is a visible, reversible edit. Round two makes the instrument deeper and the partner legible — an editor that behaves like a real IDE, a Maestro that plans, reasons, and logs every tool it runs, a cycle you can point at, and a history you can walk. The four promises the audit found unkept — provenance, time-travel, seeded generation, the clock-as-target — close here.

The editor
IDE-grade. Completion with keyboard & mouse, multi-range references, Tab-to-indent, a configurable keymap. Space is freed; F5 plays.
The Maestro
An agent, not a chatbot. It plans, runs visible tools, shows a foldable reasoning trace, takes @-mentions, and is routed by cost & latency.
The memory
Provenance on every part — prompt, seed, model, directive. A branchable musical changelog you can walk, fork, and rewind.
01

What round one got right, and where it stopped short.

The implementation audit confirmed the core is real and end-to-end: code-is-the-score with staged, auditioned, per-hunk-reversible diffs; a real-scheduler Cycle clock; deterministic offline directives; variation lanes; scenes; PANIC; keyboard-first; BYOK with local-only routing; light and dark with the colour grammar intact. The instrument plays. What it doesn’t yet do is remember, explain itself, or let you point at time.

The gaps clustered in three places. Round two takes each one as a mandate.

GAP 01

The Cycle was a read-out, not a target.

Honest, beautiful, but you couldn’t point at it. No arc-drag to scope a command; no marker for time-until-live.

GAP 02

The Maestro answered, but didn’t reason aloud.

One turn in, one shape out. No plan you could watch, no tool log, no multi-step tasks, no @-context, no completion in the score.

No provenance, no branchable history, generation counter- not seed-reproducible, no on-disk projects, simulated meters.

02
DEEPEN the Strudel editor becomes a real IDE

The Score, sharpened

Hand-coding is still sacred — nothing about typing a pattern changes. But the editor now carries its weight as an IDE: completion you can drive with the keyboard or the mouse, a selection model that lets you point the Maestro at exactly the lines you mean, Tab that indents like every other editor, and a keymap with no surprises. The transport key moved off Space so it never fights the cursor.

Three context states, never ambiguous

The Maestro always knows what you mean it to look at, and shows it. You move between three states with one key.

WHOLE FILE

Nothing pinned

The default. The Maestro reads the entire score as context. Esc always returns here.

IMPLICIT

A live cursor or drag

An ordinary text selection is the soft focus — ⌘K here edits exactly the highlighted region.

PINNED

One or many ranges

Click gutter numbers to pin ranges across the file — they persist as chips while you type, and ride into any Maestro turn.

A keymap with no collisions NEW

Space belongs to the cursor. The transport moves to F5 — a play key by every IDE convention, reachable without leaving home row reach, and impossible to type by accident. Every binding below is remappable in Settings → Keymap.

Completion is grounded in the live Strudel API — functions, your named patches & sample banks, and note literals — not a guessed token stream. Routed to the fast model so it never lags a keystroke.

03
DEEPEN from a faster pair of hands to a partner that plans

The Maestro as an agent

A one-shot directive is perfect for “make the hats swing.” But “build me an eight-bar break” is a task — several edits, in order, that have to be heard together. Round two gives the Maestro the shape of a modern coding agent: it states a plan, works it step by step, runs visible tools you can audit, and shows its reasoning when you want it. Everything it does is still a real, reversible edit to code you own — the principle is unchanged; the surface is just honest about the work.

The slash palette, opened all the way NEW

The / that summoned directives in round one is now a full, grouped command menu — generation, edits, theory, arrangement and questions in one searchable surface. Typing @ pins context inline, the same chips the gutter produces.

The plan is yours to edit

Strike a step before it runs, reorder, or stop after any one. The agent proposes a route; you keep the wheel. It never runs ahead of an audition.

Every tool is in the open

Parse, query, diff, dry-run, audition — each is a logged call with a result and a timing. There is no step that happens off-screen, and nothing reaches the speakers unvetted.

Think only when it’s worth it

Fast mode runs directives and completions on a cheap model with no trace; thinking mode spends a stronger model and shows its reasoning. You pick per turn, or let routing decide. See §09.

04
NEW a quiet, continuous layer of musical ideas

The Prompter — suggestions, whispered

The Maestro acts when asked. The Prompter only ever offers. It watches the score and the cycle and surfaces small, musical next-moves — a ghost-completion in the editor, a card that notices the pad has been static for sixteen cycles. It runs on its own role: a cheap, low-latency model (and a local one offline), because a good suggestion has to arrive faster than a thought. Nothing it proposes touches the sound until you take it.

Bounded, so it’s cheap

Suggestions are musical observations against the parsed tree — static voice, harmonic clash, empty bar — not freeform generation. That keeps them fast, on-key, and routable to a small model.

Never on the critical path

The Prompter runs off the main thread and debounces to the cycle. If the model is slow or offline, it simply goes quiet — the editor and transport never wait on it.

Learns your “no”

Dismiss a kind of suggestion and it backs off that pattern for the session. The rail is calm by design — at most three cards, and only when something is genuinely worth a glance.

05
NEW FIX honest, legible ways to see sound

Lenses on the sound

The Cycle clock answers “what is happening now.” Other questions — where is the energy, what key am I in, how dense is this pattern — want other shapes. Round two adds a set of lenses the Stage can show, alone or stacked, and lets you choose which. Each is built from real audio or the real parsed tree — never a decorative stand-in.

◷ Cycle ▤ Tracker ≋ Spectrum ⊚ Wheel ✺ Bloom ∿ Sparklines ▥ Meters filled = shown · stack any

The meters, corrected FIX

The audit was right: the v0.1 meters were too wide and their motion too sluggish — a 130 ms random-walk that read as lag, not level. Round two replaces them with compact vertical meters fed by real Web Audio analysers, at a proper ballistic response: ~60 ms attack, ~120 ms release. They take a third of the width and tell the truth.

06
NEW the promise of §05 v0.1, delivered

The Cycle becomes a target

Round one made the cycle visible and honest, but you could only read it. Now you can point at it. Drag an arc to select a span of the cycle — “the front,” “the back half,” “every off-beat” — and the next command scopes to exactly that time. And the sweep finally shows what was always implied: a marker for when a staged change goes live, so you can land it on the phrase.

Spatial language resolves to spans

“The first beat,” “every off-beat,” “the back half” — whether typed or dragged — become concrete time-spans the Maestro masks precisely.

You can hit it on the phrase

The countdown turns “it commits on a boundary” into a number you can play to. Nudge the boundary to the next 4 or 8 cycles for big moments.

The same arc, anywhere

An arc selection is a first-class context, like a gutter pin — it rides into a Maestro turn, a directive, or a Prompter suggestion identically.

07
NEW the single largest unkept promise — principles v & §09 v0.1

Provenance & time-travel

Round one’s seed message promised “every change is a diff you can read and undo” — but the only undo was the editor’s text history, and a committed part forgot how it was made. Round two makes good: every staged edit, scene, and committed lane remembers the prompt, the directive, the seed, and the model that produced it, and the project keeps a real, branchable tree of committed states you can walk, fork, and rewind. This is revision control that speaks music.

Walk it, don’t erase it

Rewind to any committed state and the transport re-evaluates to it on the next boundary — the music keeps playing while you move through time. Fork to try a different past forward.

A/B against the live mix

Two committed states can be soloed against each other the way variation lanes are — the history tree and the lane tree are the same structure, seen at two scales.

Cite your sources

Export a part with its provenance attached — the prompt and seed travel with the code. A collaborator can reproduce or remix it from the recipe alone.

08
NEW the song now lives somewhere — on disk, local-first

Projects that persist

A song was only ever in the editor buffer; close the tab and it was gone. Round two gives Refrain real projects. A project is a folder — the score, your patches and sample banks, the scene set, and the whole history tree — written to disk and autosaved as you work. Open one with ⌘O, switch between recent sets in a keystroke, and carry the entire piece on a gig laptop that owes nothing to the network.

Autosave, never a prompt

Work is written continuously; the history tree is the save file, so there is no “unsaved changes” dialog to lose a set to. Explicit ⌘S just tags a named checkpoint.

Local-first, two-tier

The desktop shell writes a real folder you can back up or version. The browser build persists to the origin-private file system — same model, same formats, smaller reach. See §10 on the platform decision.

Portable by construction

A project is plain text plus audio files. Zip it, share it, diff it in git. Open someone else’s set and the full provenance comes with it.

09
DEEPEN choose models by cost & latency, not by guesswork — extends §07.8 v0.1

Routing, made legible

Round one let you map roles to models but said nothing about which to pick. Round two makes the trade-off plain. Every model carries a cost and a latency badge, and every role carries a recommendation — because the right answer depends entirely on how often the role fires. Completions and suggestions run on every keystroke; theory runs once in a while. Spend accordingly.

A well-routed session sends roughly nine calls in ten to a cheap, fast model — and saves the strong one for the handful of moments that need real musical judgement.

10
FIX the audit’s bug and quick wins, closed out

Fixes & quick wins

CONFIRMED BUGPANIC visibly stops the clock — contradicting Fig 7.7’s promise
CAUSE

engine.panic() correctly keeps the transport alive (it evaluates silence, never stop()) — but the store also sets playing:false, and the clock freezes its sweep when not playing.

FIX

Decouple the clock’s animation from the playing flag — drive it from engine.now() directly. PANIC silences audio while the sweep keeps turning: the safest exit now looks safe.

FIX
Meters narrowed & quickened. Real Web-Audio RMS at ~60/120 ms ballistics, a third of the width, honouring reduced-motion. Master adds a true CPU% and headroom read-out. §05.
FIX
HUSH and PANIC, finally different. HUSH fades voices over a cycle for a musical exit; PANIC cuts to silence instantly. Both keep the transport — and now the clock — running.
QUICK
Variation lanes are 2–4, and refinable. The count follows the request instead of a hardcoded three, and each lane takes a follow-up (“refine the keeper”) — matching the copy v0.1 already promised.
QUICK
The Shelf is wired to disk. FILES, PACKS and PATCHES read the live project folder and sample registry instead of mock arrays. §08.
QUICK
Author your own directives. A directive is now bindable: name a verb, attach a transform, save it to a pack. The vocabulary is finally yours, as principle iv claimed.
DECIDE
Keys: keychain on desktop, encrypted-local in the browser. Resolved with the platform call below — the desktop shell uses the OS keychain; the browser tier keeps keys in an encrypted store, never in plain localStorage.
PLATFORM DECISION

Ship the desktop shell. It is the single enabler behind three of this round’s capabilities — on-disk local-first projects (§08), the OS keychain (above), and the MIDI/OSC roadmap item (§11). The browser build stays as a zero-install “try it” tier with the same formats and a smaller reach. This retires the audit’s open question rather than re-scoping the spec to browser-only.

11

Still open, after round two

With memory, agency and persistence in place, the next questions are about reach — off the laptop, onto hardware, and out to other people.

NEXT
MIDI & OSC out. Now unblocked by the desktop decision — hardware MIDI clock-out and OSC-to-SuperDirt, with the cycle as the sync master. The performance-mode label becomes a real mapping.
EXPLORE
Multiplayer on the history tree. The branchable tree is the natural substrate for collaboration — two coders committing to shared branches. Merge semantics on the cycle clock remain the hard part.
EXPLORE
A pack marketplace. Directive packs (§10) and patches as shareable, provenance-carrying bundles — a culture of vocabulary, now that authoring exists.
OPEN
What “neural generate” honestly means. The Sample Foundry’s generate tab still needs a real engine — on-device synthesis, a hosted model, or a candid relabel. Round two does not pretend it is solved.
OPEN
Notation-bridge fidelity. The Wheel lens helps, but real engraving, bidirectional staff editing, polyrhythm and microtonality are still unsolved. Where does the bridge gracefully defer to code?

A partner you can trust is one whose work you can read — the plan, the seed, the diff, and the path back.
